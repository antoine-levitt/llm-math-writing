Purpose and provenance
======================

Prepared on 5 November 2026 at the user's request. The purpose is to
compare writing while bringing the mathematical route closer to those
used by the generated papers. The user explicitly authorized changing
proof strategy, then specified that the replacement arguments should
come from the generated manuscripts and that Liouvillian notation should
be retained. The detailed setup and well-posedness arguments remain.

This is an AI-assisted adaptation combining the published paper's text
and structure with arguments from the generated papers. It is not an
untouched human manuscript or an independently generated response to the
benchmark prompt. It was prepared after the initial readability grading;
no new readability grade has been requested for it.

The source manuscript is “Coherent electronic transport in periodic
crystals”, by Eric Cancès, Clotilde Fermanian Kammerer, Antoine Levitt
and Sami Siraj-Dine:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01990v2
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-021-01026-3

The immediate editing source is prompt-version/main.tex. Its earlier
adaptations, sign conventions and provenance are documented in
NOTES.txt, statement-version-NOTES.txt and prompt-version-NOTES.txt.
All earlier baseline versions and benchmark outputs are preserved.
comparison-version-parent-hashes.json records the input hashes.

The reading bundle comparison-version/ contains only main.tex, main.pdf,
refs.bib and build.sh. These notes and the source diff are outside that
bundle. The manuscript cites only Reed–Simon, and has no explicit source
attribution or identifying PDF metadata. Its retained prose can of course
still be recognizable.

Sources of the replacement arguments
====================================

Paths below are relative to the benchmark project directory.

1. Finite-time current differentiation:
   runs/matrix-2026-11-03/explicit/output/paper.tex,
   Appendix A.3, “Differentiating the current at fixed time”.

   The proof uses the uniform H² propagator bound, Duhamel's formula with
   the free propagator on the left, the L² difference O(ε), interpolation
   to obtain an H¹ difference O(√ε), and the exact quadratic expansion of
   the current expectation. This justifies a uniform o(ε) remainder.
   The resulting first density variation is written with the retained
   Lₖ and Lₖ⁺. The original integration by parts and Hellmann–Feynman
   identities recover the baseline's response formula.

   The same general differentiation argument appears in
   runs/matrix-2026-11-03/plain/output/graphene_conductivity.tex, §4.
   Its §2.2 explicitly supplies the locally uniform H² propagator bound.
   The new version does not use the independently considered exact
   density-Duhamel proof; that approach was abandoned on user instruction.

2. Cancellation over the whole momentum torus:
   runs/matrix-2026-11-03/plain/output/graphene_conductivity.tex, §3,
   “A band sum rule at the Fermi energy”.

   The uniform sum of absolute transition matrix elements controls the
   infinitely many empty bands. The conical lower bound on the gap makes
   the filled-band Hessian integrable. Stokes' formula on the punctured
   torus, with O(r) boundary terms, cancels its full integral. This replaces
   the separate exterior/interior secular terms of the previous baseline.
   Time reversal removes the imaginary transition products, as in the
   generated papers and the previous baseline.

3. Time averaging and the conical limit:
   runs/matrix-2026-11-03/plain/output/graphene_conductivity.tex, §6,
   “The long-time Cesàro limit”.

   Time averaging produces K_T(Δ)=(1−cos(TΔ))/(TΔ³). All transitions
   with a uniformly positive gap contribute O(1/T). Near each node,
   k−kᵢ=q/T gives an integrand dominated by
   C min(|q|⁻¹,|q|⁻³), integrable in two dimensions. Dominated convergence
   reduces to the linear cone with the cutoff δ kept fixed. This replaces
   the uniform-in-time Iᴿ−Iᑫ estimate and the subsequent δ→0 limit.

   The original analytic frame, Pauli decomposition, orthogonal matrix Q,
   and explicit Dirac eigenvectors are retained to evaluate that limit.
   The elementary Laplace/Tonelli evaluation of the radial integral also
   comes from the generated paper's §6.

What is retained
================

The introduction and its oscillator example, the setting and main
statement, all of Section 3 on well-posedness, the compact Liouvillian
discussion, the spectral-projector derivative identity, the local
two-band construction, and both technical appendix proofs are retained.
Cross-references and two short scope/notation sentences in Sections 1–2
were updated. The local-model subsection only changes the argument t
of Iᵢᴿ to the averaging horizon T. The physical signs, normalization,
arbitrary fixed directions, and theorem are unchanged.

The principal new text is the finite-time differentiation proof, the
global cancellation and averaging at the beginning of Section 5, and
the rescaling in its last subsection. The complete difference against
prompt-version/main.tex is in comparison-version-changes.diff.

Notation and scope changes
===========================

• Lₖ, Lₖ⁺ and ∂βPₖ=Lₖ⁺[Pₖ,∂βHₖ] retain their previous meanings.
• The current remainder is now uniform o_T(ε), rather than C_Tε².
  No second-order Dyson expansion or quantitative long-time field
  remainder is asserted.
• Δₙₘ,ₖ=λₘ,ₖ−λₙ,ₖ and zₙₘ,ₖ abbreviate the original denominator
  and velocity product. The generated sources' product Z is the complex
  conjugate of z. Real parts agree; the Liouvillian formula retains the
  baseline's convention throughout.
• Iᵢᴿ(δ,T) now denotes the real, already time-averaged cone integral,
  with K_T inside it. Accordingly Iᴰ=π² Id/8 and the external prefactor
  is 1/(2π²), giving 1/16 per node. These symbols no longer denote the
  old instantaneous, purely imaginary integrals.
• δ is fixed and sufficiently small. The final limit uses only T→∞,
  after the previously established ε→0 limit.
