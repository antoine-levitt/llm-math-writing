             Residual conductivity at isotropic Dirac points


                                               Abstract
          We consider spinless, noninteracting electrons in a two-dimensional real periodic
      potential. Suppose that the Fermi energy meets the Bloch spectrum only at finitely many
      isolated, isotropic, two-band conical crossings. We prove that the current induced by
      a suddenly applied constant electric field, first linearized in the field strength and then
      averaged in time, has conductivity M/16 times the identity, where M is the number of
      inequivalent crossings. The proof explains the independence from the cone velocities: time
      averaging selects momenta on the inverse-time scale around each crossing, and a change
      of scale cancels the velocity factors. The conical energy assumptions also determine
      the required current matrix elements; no separate assumption on Bloch eigenvectors is
      needed.


1     The result and its mechanism
When the Fermi energy meets the bands only at isolated Dirac points, there is no Fermi
curve. A weak electric field can still drive transitions between occupied and empty states
across arbitrarily small gaps. We prove that these transitions leave a finite contribution after
a long time average, and identify why each isotropic cone contributes the same constant.
    We work in atomic units and count one spin species. The theorem is conditional on the
band structure specified below; deriving that structure from a particular lattice potential is a
separate question.
