     Residual conductivity at isotropic conical band crossings


                                             Abstract
          We consider spinless, noninteracting electrons governed by a real periodic Schrödinger
      operator in two dimensions. Suppose that the Fermi energy meets the spectrum at
      finitely many isolated, isotropic conical crossings of two adjacent bands. For a constant
      electric field switched on at time zero, we prove that the Cesàro mean of the linear-
      response conductivity is 1/16 times the identity per crossing, in atomic units. The proof
      starts from the full Schrödinger evolution. An integrated band-energy identity cancels
      the term linear in time, time reversal cancels the antisymmetric response, and a local
      two-dimensional spectral reduction determines the remaining contribution. The conical
      eigenvalue assumptions alone imply the required current matrix elements. We also justify
      the Stark dynamics, the trace per unit area, and differentiation of the unbounded current
                          1
      for potentials in Hper .


1    The model and the theorem
At an isolated conical crossing, a two-dimensional semimetal has no Fermi curve, but its
response to a weak electric field can have a nonzero long-time average. We prove this
statement for a periodic Schrödinger operator under explicit assumptions on its bands. The
order of limits is part of the result: we first take the derivative at zero field at each fixed
time, and then average that derivative over an increasing time interval.
