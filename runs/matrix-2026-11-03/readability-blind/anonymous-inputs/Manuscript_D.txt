      Residual conductivity of a periodic Schrödinger operator
                    with isotropic Dirac points



                                                 Abstract
           We prove a residual conductivity formula for a periodic Schrödinger operator in two
       dimensions, describing non-interacting spinless electrons. Its Fermi surface consists of
       finitely many isolated, isotropic, twofold conical crossings. If there are M such crossings,
       the Cesàro mean of the current response to a constant electric field, with the linear-response
       limit taken first, is M/16 times the Euclidean conductivity tensor in atomic units. The
       proof derives the response from the Stark evolution, establishes an integrable band sum
       rule that cancels the term linear in time, and reduces each crossing to a two-dimensional
       matrix calculation. In particular, the assumptions on the band energies imply the required
       velocity matrix elements; these are not additional hypotheses.

7    Consequences and scope
The value 1/16 is the contribution of a single cone for one spin species with the normalization
(1.8) and (1.1). Thus two cones give 1/8 for the spinless model; two independent identical
spin copies give 1/4. Each cone is counted once on the reciprocal torus, even if a drawing of a
Brillouin cell places representatives on several boundary edges.
    Time-reversal symmetry, supplied here by realness of V , is used to cancel the imaginary,
antisymmetric part of the response in (4.6). Isotropy is used in (5.6)–(5.3); the cone velocities
themselves need not be equal at different crossings.
    The averaging in (1.9) produces the integrable kernel (6.4). The proof therefore requires no
non-stationary phase assumption on the other bands and makes no claim that the unaveraged
response converges at large time. Likewise, it does not interchange the small-field and long-time
limits. If the Fermi gap is everywhere positive (the case of no crossings), the same proof gives
zero Cesàro limit directly from (6.1).
