        Residual conductivity from isotropic Dirac crossings


                                               Abstract
          We consider independent, spinless electrons in a two-dimensional periodic Schrödinger
      potential. Suppose that the Fermi energy meets the spectrum only at finitely many
      isolated, isotropic crossings of two bands. We prove that each crossing contributes 1/16
      to the longitudinal conductivity, in atomic units, when a constant electric field is switched
      on, linear response is taken first, and the resulting current is averaged over long times.
      The proof separates three mechanisms: cancellation of the acceleration term by the filled
      bands, determination of the leading transition currents from the conical energies alone,
      and concentration of the averaged response near the crossings. In particular, the cone
      velocities cancel from the answer. Operator domains, local traces, and the passage to
      linear response are justified for real periodic H 1 potentials.


1    The result and its mechanism
A completely filled collection of bands cannot sustain a net acceleration current: translating
the crystal momentum merely rearranges the occupied states. At a band crossing, however,
an arbitrarily weak field can couple occupied and unoccupied states with arbitrarily small
energy separation. The theorem below quantifies the current that survives this competition.
It is conditional on the band geometry; no particular lattice symmetry is required once that
geometry is assumed.

3    What an isotropic cone says about current
The hypotheses describe energies, whereas the response also involves matrix elements of
current. The bridge between them is the linear part of the Hamiltonian on the two-dimensional
crossing subspace. Isotropy forces its two directional derivatives to satisfy fixed algebraic
relations. These determine the transition strength without choosing phases for the two
eigenvectors.

A      Analytic foundations
This appendix justifies the operator statements used in the proof, including the fixed-time
differentiation before any gap estimates are invoked. The latter point is useful: the closing
gap does not obstruct linear response at a fixed time.

A.1    Domains and time evolution
In two dimensions a periodic H 1 potential is uniformly locally Lp for every finite p. Local
Sobolev estimates and a partition into enlarged lattice cells give, for every a > 0,

               ∥V f ∥2 ≤ a∥∆f ∥2 + Ca ∥f ∥2 ,          |⟨f, V f ⟩| ≤ a∥∇f ∥22 + Ca ∥f ∥22 .   (A.1)

The first inequality is also the uniformly local perturbation bound of [3, Theorem XIII.96].
Both hold on R2 and on the periodic cell. By the Kato–Rellich theorem [2, Theorem X.12],
H and Hk are self-adjoint on their H 2 domains. Their form domains are H 1 , and their
graph norms are equivalent to H 2 , uniformly for k in a bounded set. The fiber resolvents are
compact, and k 7→ Hk is analytic on the common domain. These observations also justify
the smooth spectral projections used in the main text.
   For the Stark operator, use the comparison operator

with c sufficiently large. The harmonic-oscillator graph estimate and (A.1) show that N is
self-adjoint on H 2 (R2 ) ∩ {f : |x|2 f ∈ L2 }, with core Cc∞ (R2 ). Increasing c if necessary gives
